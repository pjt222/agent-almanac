# Field Reference — forage-plants

Detailed habitat, harvesting, preparation, and Universal Five species reference extracted from `SKILL.md`.

## Critical "Never Eat" Plants (Northern Hemisphere)

| Plant | Key Identification | Danger |
|---|---|---|
| Water hemlock (*Cicuta*) | Hollow, chambered stem base; smells like carrot/parsnip; wet habitats; compound leaves | Deadly in minutes. Seizures, death. No safe dose |
| Poison hemlock (*Conium maculatum*) | Smooth stem with purple blotches; musty smell; finely divided leaves | Deadly. Ascending paralysis |
| Destroying angel / Death cap (*Amanita*) | White mushroom; white gills; ring on stem; cup (volva) at base; grows near trees | Deadly (liver failure in 3–5 days). No antidote |
| Castor bean (*Ricinus communis*) | Large star-shaped leaves; spiny seed pods | Seeds contain ricin. Deadly if chewed |
| Foxglove (*Digitalis*) | Tall spike of tubular flowers; fuzzy, wrinkled leaves | Cardiac glycosides. Heart failure |
| Nightshade family (*Solanum*, *Atropa*) | Some edible (tomato, pepper), many toxic; 5-petaled flowers; alternate leaves | Berries and foliage of wild species can be lethal |

## Habitat-to-Resource Mapping

| Habitat | Common Edible Plants | Look For |
|---|---|---|
| Open meadow / field edges | Dandelion, clover, plantain, chicory, wild onion, lamb's quarters | Sunny, disturbed ground with diverse low plants |
| Forest floor | Wood sorrel, ramps (spring), violets, fiddleheads (spring only), nuts (fall) | Dappled shade; look near logs and clearings |
| Forest edge / hedgerow | Berries (blackberry, raspberry, elderberry), rose hips, hawthorn | Transitional zone with maximum species diversity |
| Wetland / stream bank | Cattail, watercress, wild rice, arrowhead | Moist soil, standing or slow water |
| Shoreline / coastal | Seaweed (kelp, dulse, nori), sea lettuce, glasswort | Rocky intertidal zones, salt marshes |
| Disturbed ground (ruderal) | Lamb's quarters, amaranth, purslane, chickweed, stinging nettle | Trailsides, old fields, roadsides (avoid herbicide areas) |

## Harvest by Plant Part

| Plant Part | Harvest Method |
|---|---|
| Leaves | Pick individual leaves; leave at least 2/3 of the plant's foliage |
| Roots/tubers | Dig carefully; replant any root crown or small tubers to regenerate |
| Berries/fruit | Pick ripe fruit only; leave some for wildlife and seed dispersal |
| Bark/cambium | Only harvest from downed or already damaged trees; never ring-bark a living tree |
| Seeds/nuts | Collect from the ground when possible; leave enough for wildlife and regeneration |

## Preparation Methods

| Method | When to Use | How |
|---|---|---|
| Raw | Known-safe species like dandelion, wood sorrel, most berries, watercress | Wash in purified water; eat fresh |
| Boiled | Reduces bitterness, breaks down mild toxins; required for nettle, dock, fiddleheads | Boil 5–15 min; discard water for bitter plants (leaching) |
| Double-boiled (leached) | Plants with significant oxalates or tannins (acorns, dock) | Boil 10 min, discard water; boil again in fresh water |
| Roasted | Roots, tubers, seeds, nuts | Place in coals or near fire; cook until soft or dry |
| Dried | Preservation for later use; concentrates calories in seeds and roots | Air dry in sun/wind or near fire (not in direct flame) |

## The Universal Five (Beginner-Friendly Edible Plants)

1. **Dandelion (*Taraxacum officinale*)**
   - **Habitat:** Lawns, fields, disturbed ground (nearly everywhere)
   - **ID:** Basal rosette of toothed leaves; hollow stem; yellow composite flower; milky sap (exception to the milky sap rule)
   - **Edible:** Entire plant — leaves (raw/cooked), flowers (raw/fried), roots (roasted as coffee substitute)
   - **Season:** Year-round; best in spring before flowering

2. **Broadleaf Plantain (*Plantago major*)**
   - **Habitat:** Lawns, paths, disturbed ground
   - **ID:** Basal rosette of oval leaves with parallel veins; tall seed spike; tough fibrous leaves
   - **Edible:** Young leaves (raw in salads, older leaves boiled); seeds (raw or ground)
   - **Medicinal:** Crushed leaves used as poultice for insect bites/stings
   - **Season:** Spring through fall

3. **White Clover (*Trifolium repens*)**
   - **Habitat:** Lawns, meadows, roadsides
   - **ID:** Three round leaflets (sometimes four); white round flower heads; creeping ground cover
   - **Edible:** Flowers (raw or dried for tea); young leaves (raw or cooked — cook to improve digestibility)
   - **Season:** Flowers in spring/summer; leaves year-round in mild climates

4. **Cattail (*Typha latifolia* / *T. angustifolia*)**
   - **Habitat:** Wetlands, pond edges, ditches, marshes
   - **ID:** Tall (1–3 m); long flat sword-like leaves; distinctive brown cigar-shaped seed head
   - **Edible:** Shoot base/heart (raw, spring); pollen (flour substitute, summer); rhizome (starchy, peeled and boiled/roasted, year-round); young flower spike (boiled, early summer)
   - **Utility:** Fluff = tinder and insulation; leaves = weaving material
   - **Season:** Different parts edible year-round

5. **Wood Sorrel (*Oxalis* spp.)**
   - **Habitat:** Forest floor, shaded areas, gardens
   - **ID:** Three heart-shaped leaflets (notched, unlike clover); small 5-petaled yellow, white, or pink flowers; leaves fold at night
   - **Edible:** Leaves and flowers (raw — pleasant lemony/sour taste)
   - **Caution:** Contains oxalic acid; eat in moderation (not as a staple)
   - **Season:** Spring through fall

**Progression:** Master these 5 → add 5 regional species → add 5 more → build to 20+ positively known species.
