## Pin Placement Reference (Step 4)

```text
Pin Placement by Order:
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+
| Order              | Pin Position                             |
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+
| Coleoptera         | Through the RIGHT ELYTRON (front wing    |
| (beetles)          | cover), approximately 1/3 from the       |
|                    | anterior edge, so the pin emerges        |
|                    | between the middle and hind legs.        |
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+
| Lepidoptera        | Through the CENTER OF THE THORAX         |
| (butterflies/moths)| (mesothorax), between the wing bases.    |
|                    | Wings must be spread on a spreading      |
|                    | board before the specimen dries.         |
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+
| Hymenoptera        | Through the RIGHT SIDE OF THE THORAX     |
| (bees/wasps/ants)  | (mesothorax), between the wing bases.    |
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+
| Diptera            | Through the RIGHT SIDE OF THE THORAX     |
| (flies)            | (mesothorax), between the wing bases.    |
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+
| Hemiptera          | Through the RIGHT SIDE OF THE            |
| (true bugs)        | SCUTELLUM (triangular plate between      |
|                    | wing bases), slightly to the right of    |
|                    | center.                                  |
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+
| Orthoptera         | Through the RIGHT SIDE OF THE            |
| (grasshoppers)     | PRONOTUM (just behind the head), to      |
|                    | the right of the midline.                |
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+
| Odonata            | Through the CENTER OF THE THORAX.        |
| (dragonflies)      | Wings must be spread. Alternatively,     |
|                    | store in glassine envelopes.             |
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+
| All other orders   | Through the RIGHT SIDE OF THE THORAX     |
|                    | unless order-specific guidance is        |
|                    | available.                               |
+--------------------+------------------------------------------+

Pin Selection:
- Standard entomological pins: stainless steel, sizes 0-7
- Size 3 (0.50mm) is the most commonly used general-purpose size
- Size 1-2 for small beetles and flies; size 4-5 for large beetles
- Specimens under 5mm: mount on a paper point (triangular card
  glued to a standard pin) rather than pinning directly

Pin Height:
- The specimen should sit approximately 2/3 up the pin (leaving
  room below for 2 labels and above for handling)
- Use a pinning block (stepped block with 3 heights) to ensure
  consistent specimen and label heights across the collection

Spreading Wings (Lepidoptera, Odonata):
1. Pin the specimen through the thorax
2. Place on the spreading board with the body in the groove
3. Use paper strips to hold wings in position
4. Adjust wings so the hind margin of the forewing is perpendicular
   to the body axis
5. Leave on the board for 3-7 days until completely dry
6. Remove paper strips carefully
```

## Collection Methods (Step 2)

| Method | Best For & Procedure |
|---|---|
| Sweep net | Flying and vegetation-dwelling insects in grasslands, meadows, and low shrubs. Sweep in a figure-8 pattern through vegetation; empty net into a killing jar or collecting bag after every 10–20 sweeps |
| Beating tray | Arboreal insects on trees and shrubs. Hold a white sheet or tray under a branch; strike sharply 3–5 times; collect dislodged insects with an aspirator or forceps |
| Pitfall trap | Ground-dwelling insects (beetles, ants, crickets). Bury cup flush with soil surface, add rain cover, check every 24–48 hours. Use propylene glycol as preservative (non-toxic to mammals; never ethylene glycol) |
| Light trap | Nocturnal flying insects (moths, beetles, lacewings). White sheet illuminated by mercury vapor or UV light from dusk to midnight/dawn; best on warm, humid, moonless nights |
| Malaise trap | Flying insects, especially Hymenoptera and Diptera. Tent-like mesh barrier intercepting flight into a collecting head with preservative; runs continuously, check weekly |
| Aspirator (pooter) | Small, delicate insects that cannot be handled with forceps. Mouth-operated tube with mesh filter to prevent inhalation |
| Pan trap | Pollinators and small flying insects. Colored bowls (yellow, white, blue) with soapy water at vegetation height; check every 24–48 hours |
| Hand collection | Large, slow, or sessile insects (bark beetles, caterpillars on host plants, aquatic larvae under rocks) using forceps or fingers |

## Dispatch Methods (Step 3)

| Method | Procedure and Notes |
|---|---|
| Ethyl acetate killing jar | Absorbent material (plaster of Paris or tissue) in bottom of wide-mouth jar saturated with ethyl acetate; death in 1–5 minutes for most species. Flammable and irritant — use in well-ventilated areas, do not inhale |
| Freezing | Place live insects in a container at -20 °C for 24 hours. Produces well-relaxed specimens ideal for pinning |
| Ethanol drowning | Immerse directly in 70–95% ethanol for soft-bodied wet-preserved insects (larvae, aphids, small Diptera). Never use for Lepidoptera (destroys scales) or dry-pinning specimens |

**Never use:** Cyanide jars (extremely toxic, obsolete), crushing/squeezing (destroys morphology), or prolonged suffocation (slow and damages specimens from struggling).

## Wet Preservation Protocol (Step 5)

| Category | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Preservative | 70–80% ethanol for morphological study; 95–100% ethanol for DNA-grade preservation (change ethanol after 24 hours to remove body-fluid dilution) |
| Suitable specimens | Larvae (caterpillars, grubs, maggots), soft-bodied adults (aphids, termites, some small Diptera), aquatic insects, immature stages (nymphs, pupae) |
| Containers | Screw-cap glass vials or polyethylene snap-cap vials. Avoid rubber stoppers (ethanol dissolves them). Label goes INSIDE the vial |
| Fluid ratio | At least 3 parts preservative to 1 part specimen volume |
| Long-term storage | Check fluid levels every 6–12 months; top up as needed; store cool and dark |

**Do NOT use:** Formaldehyde/formalin for routine preservation (destroys DNA, health hazards) or isopropanol (excessive hardening and color loss).

## Storage & Curation Standards (Steps 7–8)

### Dry Collection Storage

| Component | Standard |
|---|---|
| Unit trays | Cardboard or plastic trays with plastazote foam bottoms; specimens pinned in organized rows |
| Drawers | Tight-fitting dust- and pest-excluding drawers (glass-topped allows viewing without opening) |
| Cabinets | Steel cabinets with tight compressed-air gasket seals |
| Pest management | Pest strips (DDVP) or naphthalene/paradichlorobenzene crystals in each drawer; replace every 6 months to prevent museum beetle (*Anthrenus*) and book lice (*Psocoptera*) infestations |
| Climate control | 40–50% relative humidity, 18–22 °C; avoid fluctuations and direct sunlight |

**Wet storage:** Store screw-cap glass vials upright in racks in a cool, dark, ventilated area away from ignition sources; check and top up ethanol every 6–12 months.

### Curation Schedule & Database Fields

| Task | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Pest inspection | Every 3–6 months (check for frass, cast skins, live pests) |
| Fumigant refresh | Every 6 months |
| Ethanol top-up | Every 6–12 months for wet collections |
| Repair | As needed: re-pin loose specimens, re-glue appendages with water-soluble PVA glue, replace damaged labels |
| Identification updates | Add new determination labels below old ones — never remove prior determination labels |

**Minimum database fields (Darwin Core / WGS84 decimal degrees / ISO 8601 `YYYY-MM-DD`):** unique catalog number, taxon (order/family/genus/species), locality & coordinates, collection date, collector, collection method, determiner & determination date, storage location, preservation type.
